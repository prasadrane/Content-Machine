## Active Governed Rules (Seeded from Golden Post #1)
1. **Rule 1 — Strict Word Count Limit (120–280 words)**: Never write lengthy multi-section posts or essays. Keep posts tightly bounded between 120 and 280 words with 1–2 sentence paragraph spacing. (Provenance: anchor-post-01, 2026-09-07)
2. **Rule 2 — Developer Flow & Cognitive Load**: Anchor tool comparisons and technical insights in tangible cognitive flow (e.g., tool latency, waiting around, drifting to Slack, losing the thread, rebuilding mental models) rather than dry feature lists. (Provenance: anchor-post-01, 2026-09-07)
3. **Rule 3 — Zero Engagement Bait / No Cheesy CTAs**: Conclude posts with an honest, balanced reflection or personal verdict. Never tack on artificial engagement questions like 'What do you think?', 'Agree?', or 'Drop a comment below!'. (Provenance: anchor-post-01, 2026-09-07)
4. **Rule 4 — Zero Fabricated Production or Payment Outages**: Ground all technical arguments in hands-on building, local agentic runs, and test harness boundaries. Never invent corporate production crashes (e.g. 'crashed production at 3 AM', payment webhook outages, billing API failures) to manufacture drama. (Provenance: user-correction, 2026-09-07)
5. **Rule 5 — Ban "Broetry" Staccato Cadence**: Group related premise, mechanics, and friction into cohesive mini-paragraphs (2–3 sentences). Do not isolate every single sentence into an artificial one-line paragraph block unless presenting an imperative checklist. (Provenance: ai-reviewer-feedback, 2026-09-07)
6. **Rule 6 — Ban "X isn't Y, it's Z" Fortune-Cookie Aphorisms**: Avoid the formulaic antithetical mic-drop template (e.g., *"Velocity without boundaries isn't speed, it's delayed debugging"*). Conclude on an unvarnished statement of technical mechanics or model behavior. (Provenance: ai-reviewer-feedback, 2026-09-07)
7. **Rule 7 — Strict 2–3 Hashtags Limit**: Keep hashtags strictly to 2 or 3 hyper-relevant technical tags at the bottom. Never exceed 3 tags. (Provenance: ai-reviewer-feedback, 2026-09-07)
8. **Rule 8 — Visceral Developer Pain Over Abstract Metaphors**: Anchor friction in tangible engineer realities (e.g. *"spending half your Friday debugging edge cases"*, *"scheduling an emergency debugging session for later"*) rather than abstract metaphors like *"the hidden tax"* or *"delayed debugging"*. (Provenance: prasad-edit, 2026-09-07)
9. **Rule 9 — Concrete Technical Specificity Over Generic Hand-Waving**: Must cite concrete failure modes (HTTP 429s, partial responses, payload validation edge cases, schema drift) rather than vague generalities like 'debugging edge cases'. Grounding in lived technical details is the single most effective humanizer. (Provenance: ai-reviewer-feedback-02, 2026-09-07)
10. **Rule 10 — Aphorism Density Cap (Maximum 1 Takeaway Per Post)**: Never chain consecutive LinkedIn epigrams or quotable soundbites. Consecutive aphorisms sound like an AI trying to write thought leadership. Keep prose conversational and observational. (Provenance: ai-reviewer-feedback-02, 2026-09-07)
11. **Rule 11 — Ban Rhetorical Contrast Crutches**: Ban LLM rhetorical contrast formulas ('X feels fast until Y...', 'You aren't saving X, you're Y...', 'The problem isn't X, it's how we Y...'). State observations directly without dramatic rhetorical packaging. (Provenance: ai-reviewer-feedback-02, 2026-09-07)
12. **Rule 12 — Ban Manufactured Metaphors**: Ban dramatic LinkedIn clichés like 'stops the bleeding', 'bleeding edge', or 'pure velocity'. Use plain practitioner phrasing like 'What works better for me:'. (Provenance: ai-reviewer-feedback-02, 2026-09-07)
13. **Rule 13 — Conversational Practitioner Field Note Cadence**: Frame posts from a senior engineer's direct experience ('I've started treating...', 'I've seen agents...', 'What works better for me:'). Slightly messier and grounded, rather than an immaculate, sanitized lecture. (Provenance: ai-reviewer-feedback-02, 2026-09-07)
14. **Rule 14 — Natural Conversational Closers (No Formulaic 'Lesson:' or 'Rule:' Prefixes)**: Conclude posts with organic practitioner observations or direct takeaways. Never prepend artificial labels like 'Lesson:', 'Rule:', or 'Takeaway:' to the closing sentence. In authentic engineering writing, the technical punchline integrates seamlessly into the final thought. (Provenance: post-audit-01, 2026-09-07)
