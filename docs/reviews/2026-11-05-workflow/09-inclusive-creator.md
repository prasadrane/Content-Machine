# Inclusive creator workflow review

Date: 2026-11-05. Independent veteran product-owner and inclusive-UX critique; planning only.
Verdict: support the direction conditionally. One private, recoverable content workflow can serve busy technical creators; a compulsory wizard, repeated consent and profile setup would undermine it.
Method: read the brief/protocol, all five specialist reports and the proposed plan; no other whole-product report was read. Specialist source evidence is reused below, without another broad code audit.
Graph orientation completed; coverage for these seven documents reported metadata changes, so their full live text supplied the review evidence. No browser/accessibility test, live generation or representative-user study was performed.
Confirmed requirements govern this review: saved-work landing with prominent Resume; equal personal/discovery starts; one destination first; skippable AI review; required human approval; manual publishing; Instagram captions AND Reel scripts; engineering audience.

## U1 — Keep / Simplify: saved work is the front door
Why: returning creators need their work, while first-time creators need an obvious beginning rather than a productivity dashboard.
Current evidence: I11, N3-N6 distinguish session continuity, source history and real saved items; their earlier return-choice questions are superseded by the brief.
Proposed interaction: show actual saved ideas/drafts, a prominent Resume action naming its item, and New content. New content offers equally weighted own-notes and discovery entries; existing drafts enter directly through own content. Resume is deliberate, never an automatic replacement.
How to validate: a new empty workspace needs no profile/source setup; a returning creator opens either of two identically titled items by distinguishable details and gets the correct saved text/status.

## U2 — Simplify: four stages describe work, not four compulsory tasks
Why: an author with a finished draft should not repeat intake or spend attention proving that they visited a stage.
Current evidence: I4-I5, D1 and N1-N2 expose unnecessary interview/tool handoffs; the plan correctly proposes one active region.
Proposed interaction: retain the four confirmed stages, with plain-language labels proposed separately. Keep one editor and a compact current-stage indicator; expose sources/history on demand. Permit revisiting stages; gate approval/export on actual prerequisites, never on visited pages. Remove guides and competing navigation.
How to validate: paste a draft, skip AI review, approve and export without a scan, interview, tutorial, redundant editor or hidden next action.

## U3 — Simplify / Remove: context helps authors without interrogating them
Why: a technical creator may offer analysis of an article without implementation experience, metrics or an incident story.
Current evidence: I5-I6, D2 and E11 show assumed personal experience, lost context and templates demanding evidence that may not exist.
Proposed interaction: keep source attachments separate from editable author notes/interpretation and pass both to writing requests. Offer optional help only when requested or a specific generation gap needs resolution. Reject mandatory anecdote/metric prompts and an extra “no personal example” acknowledgment; leaving experience absent is sufficient.
How to validate: source-only commentary stays attributed; a shorter draft is acceptable; unanswered prompts produce no invented number, employer, outage or first-person accomplishment.

## U4 — Simplify: optional AI advice has no permission-giving score
Why: the creator needs useful edits and a clear choice, not a grade or an explanation for declining help.
Current evidence: D4-D8 and I8-I10 identify score competition, magic wording claims and successful-looking fallbacks.
Proposed interaction: offer Review draft and explicit Skip AI review, without a reason field. Lead feedback with the passage and suggested change; keep one optional wording action with comparison. Remove PASS/PEAK prestige, “viral”, “humanized” and verification claims; diagnostics stay secondary.
How to validate: a failed request remains failed until the creator deliberately retries or continues without review; skipped, current and earlier-version review remain distinguishable, and none proves factual accuracy.

## U5 — Simplify: the approval action is the human acknowledgment
Why: reading the actual deliverable and deliberately approving it provides the required decision; a checkbox repeating the same consent increases effort without explaining more.
Current evidence: D10 and E1-E3 preserve exact-version approval, while E3 and the plan propose acknowledgment plus approval.
Proposed interaction: show the full destination/format deliverable, honest review state and expandable sources/changes; use one explicit “Approve this version” action. Reject an additional attestation checkbox, duplicate modal or stage-4 confirmation. Record the exact version, destination, format and text approved.
How to validate: approval works after an explicit AI skip; changing even one character invalidates working approval; historical approved text remains available; restoring identical text as a new version does not revive consent.

## U6 — Move / Simplify: dictation belongs to the intended field
Why: speaking must reduce entry effort without forcing a separate Audio journey or risking existing text.
Current evidence: I7, D9 and S10 describe field targeting, delayed transcripts and different transcription paths.
Proposed interaction: labeled Dictate/Stop controls sit beside notes/draft intake; show recording, transcribing, cancellation and failure locally. Preserve typed text and the originating item/version/field/insertion point; offer an editable transcript for insertion if context changed. Explain the actual processing path; private workspace does not mean fully local transcription.
How to validate: denied permission leaves typing usable; keyboard start/stop works; leaving releases the microphone; a late transcript cannot enter another item or overwrite intervening edits.

## U7 — Keep / Add: accessibility follows the whole journey
Why: reduced navigation is useful only when people can locate, read, operate and recover their work with their chosen input method.
Current evidence: I12, D7, E5/E10 and N12-N13 supply semantic/focus foundations and outstanding journey checks.
Proposed interaction: keep warm cm-* styling; use semantic links/buttons, associated field labels, visible focus and text status. Brand icons accompany visible destination names; redundant graphics are hidden from assistive technology. Give comparisons a linear text reading order; announce meaningful outcomes without rereading the draft or announcing every keystroke.
How to validate: keyboard and screen-reader paths retain location/focus after history, errors and Settings; forced colors remain meaningful; 320px reflow and 200% text resize with the phone keyboard keep text/actions reachable without stacked sticky bars.

## U8 — Add / Simplify: preservation is the shared recovery contract
Why: lost thought costs more than a failed model call; ambiguous “Saved” or empty-library states make recovery harder.
Current evidence: I9/I11, D8, N4-N11 and S8-S9 show why local component state, swallowed failures and seed refreshes cannot establish durable private work.
Proposed interaction: preserve exact input across stages, Settings, history and retries; show Saving/Saved/Not saved from real acknowledgments. Distinguish successful empty, no matches, unavailable and partial results, keeping usable prior content with its timestamp. Bind every delayed response to its original item/version; retry never replaces current work silently.
How to validate: save/load failure retains work and truthful status; retry does not duplicate items; a delayed result for A cannot alter B. Only successful durable recovery permits a Resume promise; hosted ownership applies to records, history and files.

## U9 — Keep / Simplify: export ends with an unchanged, usable artifact
Why: the creator wants something to publish manually, with an escape route if clipboard or download fails.
Current evidence: E1-E9 and N14 distinguish approved content from later rewriting, server files and unsupported publication claims.
Proposed interaction: complete all writing before approval; export the exact artifact with named Copy, Download and selectable text. Choose destination then supported format; Instagram offers both Caption and Reel script as separate choices, without forcing a bundle. Scripts are text with useful production cues, never a rendered video. Adaptation is optional and separately approvable.
How to validate: caption never returns a generic script; Reel script preserves cues; X has per-post boundaries; clipboard denial offers selection/download without false success; export triggers no model call and never claims Published. A failed adaptation leaves the original exportable.

## U10 — Move / Simplify: supporting views earn their place through control
Why: reusable preferences reduce repeated edits; factual profile/source settings control context. Neither should become onboarding or interrupt a finished piece.
Current evidence: S1-S9 support optional Lessons and real Settings; the plan/S5 also place writing-preference editing inside Settings, risking duplicate management.
Proposed interaction: Lessons owns one editable preference list, with Add/Edit/Stop using and explicit approval of AI suggestions; Settings links to that same list and owns optional author facts, defaults and configured sources. No mandatory profile, extraction tool, completion prompt or repeated save confirmation. Keep factual item corrections out of general preferences.
How to validate: complete the first piece without either supporting view; a blocked preference save retains text and shows the conflict; changing a preference affects future requests without rewriting approved artifacts.

## Cross-view decisions and contradictions

- U1 resolves I11/D10/E9/N3's obsolete startup questions: saved list plus prominent, deliberate Resume is confirmed; do not ask again or automatically reopen the latest editor.
- U5 changes E3's extra acknowledgment proposal: the explicit version-bound approval action supplies human consent; optional AI never supplies or blocks that consent.
- U9 resolves E4's conditional Instagram support: captions and Reel scripts are release requirements, requiring distinct truthful implementations; a generic script cannot satisfy either by relabeling.
- U10 rejects two writing-preference editors; one underlying list and contextual links are sufficient. Defer S2's automated edit-to-lesson extraction rather than inserting another post-export decision.

## Smallest coherent release

Ship saved private items/Resume, equal intake routes, one platform/format draft, optional AI review, exact human approval and unchanged manual export, including both required Instagram formats. Keep direct existing-draft entry and safe contextual dictation.
Persistence/ownership, source-versus-author context, comparison, truthful failures and complete keyboard/mobile operation are release requirements across every view, not a later accessibility pass. Preserve in-session continuity during replacement; do not delete historical user or retired Comments data.
Lessons may remain a small optional preference editor; Settings needs only real optional author facts/defaults/sources. Remove synthetic personas/scores/replays, demo noise, decorative emoji, duplicated polish and guide controls in the same coherent pass.
Defer elaborate onboarding, judge/score dashboards, advanced filters, automatic learning, bulk bundles, team/public workspaces, automated publishing and image/video production. These add no prerequisite for the confirmed manual finish.

## End-to-end acceptance cases — proposed, not executed

1. First-time keyboard user: empty private list → New content → own notes → Instagram Caption → type/edit → explicit AI skip → read/approve → copy. No profile, sources, metric, tutorial or extra consent is demanded.
2. Discovery user: configured source partially fails → successful items remain → choose attributed article → offer interpretation without personal example → review/compare → approve/export; no invented lived experience or fake score appears.
3. Returning screen-reader user: Resume names the saved item → restore acknowledged text/stage → open another item and Back → history comparison/cancel returns focus correctly; an unavailable list says failure rather than “No saved content”.
4. Voice/mobile user: type notes → dictate → Stop → navigate while transcribing → return to the originating field and inspect transcript before insertion; denied permission, silence or cancellation loses no typed text.
5. Interrupted author: save fails during editing → exact text stays visible with Retry and an explicitly unapproved backup-text option → successful save/restart restores that version; no stale fetch or late review overwrites newer input.
6. Independent approvals: approve a Caption → adapt to Reel script with production cues → edit/skip or run review/approve separately; editing, failure or cancellation never changes the approved caption or borrows its approval.
7. Manual finish: approve a segmented X thread → deny clipboard permission → select text or download the same approved posts; truthful feedback reports only the actual action, with no rewrite, upload or publication claim.
8. Supporting detour: edit an unsaved source/preference → encounter validation/conflict → return to Workflow and back without losing input; another creator cannot read any item, draft, history, approval or export.

Unresolved creator decisions: none that block this proposal; all six recorded product questions have answers. Labels and implementation choices remain reviewable proposals, not additional onboarding questions for the creator.
