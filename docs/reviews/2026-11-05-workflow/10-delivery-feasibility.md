# Whole-product delivery feasibility review

Date: 2026-11-05. Role: pragmatic product owner and UX engineering lead. Planning only.
Verdict: conditionally feasible; approve the product direction, revise the delivery contract before implementation.
The smallest complete product lets a private technical creator return to saved work, develop one real deliverable, approve it and take it away unchanged.
This is independent AI expert critique, not representative-user research, browser validation or a delivery estimate.
Evidence: read the brief, all five specialist reports and the provisional workflow plan; reuse their source-verified findings rather than repeat their audits.
Repository graph orientation was completed; no new structural absence claim depends on the graph.

## R1 — Keep: one workflow with four distinct jobs
Why: choose material, develop it, authorize it and obtain it are meaningful creator decisions; subsystem navigation is not.
Current evidence: N1/N2 identify six tool destinations and location-based progress; E1/E2 show writing continuing after anchor approval.
Proposed interaction: one active stage inside Workflow; Lessons and Settings support it. Keep approval and export as compact separate regions, with automatic advance after approval.
Remove the guide toggle, demo navigation, standalone Audio, trophy/peak competition and duplicate polish controls; preserve contextual dictation, comparison and the shared editorial theme (I7/I12, D5-D7, N9).
How to validate: an existing draft reaches manual export without an interview, score dashboard, Settings visit or second approval of unchanged content.

## R2 — Simplify: saved work first; platform choice belongs to New content
Why: forcing returning creators through a platform picker would contradict the confirmed saved-list/Resume entry and risk replacing current work.
Current evidence: N3-N6 describe absent durable item recovery; I1/I2 propose platform-first intake. Older reports' open startup questions are superseded by the brief.
Proposed interaction: open the real saved-item list with prominent Resume and New content. Resume restores that item's platform; New content chooses one destination, then presents equally prominent own-notes and discovery starts.
Existing draft is immediate input under the own-content start; an empty library gives the same two starts without fabricated records. Discovery results remain source history, not automatically saved creator ideas.
How to validate: two same-title items resume independently; no sources/profile are required to paste a draft; a failed library load shows Retry rather than an empty successful library.

## R3 — Simplify: intake supplies evidence and intent, not an invented life story
Why: a technical creator can add a useful interpretation without claiming implementation, benchmarks or a personal incident.
Current evidence: I4-I6/I10 find forced interviews, dropped excerpt context and successful-looking fallback briefings; D2 finds editor notes excluded from revision requests.
Proposed interaction: keep notes/draft, source attachment and intended point visible; optional help asks for missing context. Send source evidence, author contribution and interpretation through every writing request.
Move configured-source maintenance to Settings; discovery keeps refresh and Manage sources. Preserve typed notes when recording, changing source or cancelling help; disclose the actual transcription path.
How to validate: a sourced article with no personal example yields attributed commentary; provider failure preserves inputs and produces no invented tension, metric, anecdote or unrelated replay.

## R4 — Add: develop the final format before asking for approval
Why: reviewing an anchor and generating the publishable format afterward makes the creator approve the wrong object.
Current evidence: D1/D3 locate drafting outside the main editor and missing platform context; E2/E11 identify post-approval rewriting and templates demanding unsupported evidence.
Proposed interaction: stage 2 owns one platform-specific editor, Create draft when needed, Review draft and one optional wording suggestion. Pasted drafts stay intact until an accepted change.
AI review is optional; not run, completed for this version, failed, earlier-version and explicitly skipped are distinct. A failure offers Retry or an explicit skip; it never silently records a skip or a pass.
How to validate: each supported format reaches approval already complete; suggestions use a comparison, cancellation changes nothing, and accepting altered bytes creates a new unapproved version.

## R5 — Add: an explicit first-release capability contract
Why: an internal vertical slice can be narrow; the advertised first release must honor existing capabilities and the confirmed Instagram addition.
Current evidence: E4/E6/E11 distinguish LinkedIn posts, X threads, generic short-video scripts, YouTube long-form scripts and newsletter drafts; Instagram caption/Reel support is not established by the generic prompt.
Proposed interaction: inventory and map every existing output choice before changing the picker; retain those capabilities with honest labels. First release additionally supports both Instagram captions and Reel scripts as selectable deliverables.
Do not silently remove the generic short-script path or call it validated Instagram/TikTok/Shorts support. Resolve its destination/format mapping during adapter discovery; never promise rendered media, account connection or publication.
Prefer one selected artifact, not a compulsory caption-plus-script bundle. A different format/destination creates a linked unapproved draft; adaptation stays optional after the first handoff.
How to validate: a release matrix exercises every retained output and both Instagram choices; X has explicit post boundaries/limits; scripts preserve production cues and never require invented benchmark sections.

## R6 — Keep: human approval authorizes the actual portable artifact
Why: the creator needs a concrete result to inspect before deciding what may be presented as final.
Current evidence: E1-E3/E6/E8 show useful exact-session checks but transformed derivatives and unguarded Copy; N14 distinguishes export from publication.
Proposed interaction: prepare the full post/thread/caption/script/newsletter in stage 2; stage 3 previews that exact platform/format/version with honest review status and one explicit acknowledgment.
Stage 4 reads the immutable approved artifact and offers Copy/Download plus selectable-text recovery; it performs no generation, cleanup or conversion. Editing/restoring creates another unapproved version.
How to validate: one-space edit and platform change revoke working approval; export matches approved content under a documented encoding contract; denied clipboard access never reports Copied or Published.

## R7 — Add: private durable records and non-destructive migration as a release gate
Why: saved-list/Resume and editable real data are core product promises, not later operational polish.
Current evidence: N4/N5 and E9 find session-only approval, incomplete lineage and slug-based file overwrite; S8 finds demo seeding and runtime Markdown refresh from repository seeds.
Proposed interaction: persist owned items, evidence, versions, actual review/approval associations, preferences and sources; acknowledge Saved only after the write succeeds. Same titles and repeated exports must not collide.
Reuse the local-first runtime for an internal slice; a hosted private release additionally needs authenticated server-side ownership on every record/history/artifact route. Select auth/storage vendors only after implementation discovery.
Migrate without deleting historical user/Comments data; quarantine provable demo records by provenance, preserve uncertain records, and label unmatched legacy approvals/lineage unavailable. Seed changes cannot overwrite creator edits.
How to validate: restart/deploy recovers acknowledged work; failed saves retain text; creator B cannot read or mutate A's items/files through direct identifiers; migration/redeployment preserves edited settings and historical exports.

## R8 — Move: configuration and reusable preferences into simple supporting views
Why: creators need editable context and fewer repeated corrections, without a second publishing journey or two competing preference banks.
Current evidence: S1/S3-S7/S9 find optional lessons value, false activation success, missing edit/stop controls, hardcoded identity and overwrite-prone refresh.
Proposed interaction: Settings owns optional author facts and saved discovery sources; Lessons owns reusable writing preferences with Add/Edit/Stop using and actual save/conflict outcomes. Settings links to the same preference records.
Keep universal grounding/approval rules separate from personal preferences. AI suggestions remain pending until explicitly accepted; current approved artifacts never change when future-writing preferences change.
How to validate: a blank creator has no invented identity; changing a source preserves others; failed/conflicting saves retain exact input; a stopped preference stays inactive after restart; Workflow remains available throughout.

## R9 — Keep: safe movement and asynchronous ownership throughout the journey
Why: creators will edit, pause, open another item and visit Settings while work is pending.
Current evidence: D7/D8, E9/E10 and N6/N7/N10 preserve useful request snapshots but identify unsafe restoration and incomplete routing/recovery.
Proposed interaction: item/version/field identity travels with requests; late results offer Open/Compare for their original context. Stable private item/stage routes and read-only historical previews replace direct overwrites.
Retain current in-session continuity until tested replacement exists. Stop recording on exit; do not promise reload-surviving background generation unless implemented. Save text independently of running AI.
How to validate: delay review/transcription on A, edit A and open B; neither newer text nor B changes. Back/Forward and Settings return preserve context without rerunning requests; Restore creates a new revision.

## R10 — Simplify: sequence by complete creator outcomes and shared contracts
Why: parallel redesign of old tabs would preserve broken handoffs and create competing item models.
Current evidence: I11, D2/D3, E9, N4/N5 and S8 share the same missing identity/context/preservation dependencies; these are one foundation, not five independent patches.
First: one foundation owner defines record/API contracts, migration, ownership and save/approval semantics; record unresolved hosting/vendor choices as discovery work, without introducing queues, event buses or new orchestration frameworks.
Next: prove one local internal journey with an existing LinkedIn draft, explicit AI skip, durable save/resume, exact approval and real download. This is an internal integration checkpoint, not the advertised platform release.
Then parallel owners build (a) shell/intake/discovery/dictation, (b) writing/context/review and format adapters, (c) simple Settings/Lessons, and (d) approval/export/accessibility integration against those contracts.
Centralize shared schema/storage/API and shell-registry edits with named owners; feature owners consume interfaces and retain others' changes. Finish with the full capability matrix, hosted privacy gate when applicable, and non-destructive migration rehearsal.
How to validate: use TDD for implementation, backend/frontend regression suites and build, then real browser journey/accessibility checks; source review and green unit tests alone do not establish completed product delivery.

## R11 — Add: decisive end-to-end release scenarios
Why: a coherent release is established by finishing and recovering real work, not by separately rendering the new views.
Acceptance: new empty creator -> own notes/dictation or existing draft -> target format -> explicit AI skip -> human approval -> exact copy/download; no profile/source setup or synthetic identity appears.
Acceptance: configured discovery -> source excerpt plus author interpretation -> generation -> reviewed revision comparison -> approval -> export; a failed connector preserves partial successes and a failed model offers manual continuation.
Acceptance: saved item A -> Settings edits -> item B -> restart -> Resume A; text, sources, version and actual approval recover, while failed loads/saves remain visibly different from empty/saved states.
Acceptance: approved LinkedIn item -> Instagram caption and separate Reel-script adaptations -> independent approval/export; cancellation, failure and preference changes leave the original approved artifact available.
Acceptance: run the retained-platform matrix plus keyboard/screen-reader completion, 200% text resize and 320px reflow; labeled icons, focus return, announced errors and full readable comparisons work across all stages.

## Release boundary and remaining decisions
Must have: R1-R9 contracts and interactions, all retained formats plus both Instagram choices, optional adaptation isolation, real copy/download and R11 recovery/privacy/accessibility gates.
Defer: inferred-lesson extraction from version pairs, advanced taxonomy/score dashboards, rich platform replicas, automatic publishing, media rendering, teams, public showcase, offline synchronization and new connectors.
Reject: fake successful fallbacks, fixed author personas, score-gated human approval, post-approval rewriting, automatic seed overwrite and infrastructure introduced without a demonstrated runtime need.
No unanswered creator product question remains among the six confirmed answers. Stage microcopy, generic-script mapping and hosting/auth/storage choices need design/implementation discovery, not reopening those answers.
No product code, data, test execution, browser session, external action or deployment was changed by this review.
